# Design Principles

## Visual Hierarchy

* Preserve New Source branding and site-wide animated background treatment.
* Improve spacing, alignment, typography, and content hierarchy.
* Keep primary actions visually distinct.
* Avoid oversized category imagery.
* Reserve stronger visual emphasis for product-level content.

## Navigation

* Give search and category browsing equal prominence.
* Keep direct SKU and product-name retrieval fast.
* Preserve clear brand navigation.
* Minimize unnecessary navigation depth.
* Keep account, cart, service, and contact access consistently visible.

## Professional Buyer Focus

* Optimize for users who often know the product, brand, or application they need.
* Prioritize fast scanning over decorative presentation.
* Keep product cards compact and information-dense.
* Make filtering and sorting persistent and easy to use.
* Avoid guided-selling elements that interrupt direct purchase paths.

## Catalog Presentation

Use a grid/list hybrid that supports:

* Product image
* Product name
* SKU
* Brand
* Availability
* Price
* Key specifications
* Quick comparison
* Add-to-cart action where appropriate

## Filtering Priority

1. Category
2. Application / use case
3. Compatibility
4. Brand
5. Product specifications

## Inventory Presentation

Normal availability:

`In Stock`

Low inventory:

`Only X remaining`

Out of stock:

* Disable normal purchase
* Preserve product page
* Provide product-specific contact options
* Display available alternatives

Pre-order:

* Maintain a distinct pre-order state

## Product Pages

Product-detail pages should emphasize:

* Product identity
* Price
* Availability
* Purchase action
* Specifications
* Application information
* Compatibility notes
* Related products

## Reorder

Authenticated retail customers should be able to reach prior purchases quickly.

Reorder should:

* Validate current inventory
* Use current Lightspeed pricing
* Enter the standard reservation and checkout flow

## Out-of-Stock Recovery

Out-of-stock status should create a secondary sales path rather than a dead end.

Primary recovery options:

* Email inquiry
* Phone contact
* Customers Also Chose
* Replacement or related products

## Customer Experience

Customer-experience improvements should remain subordinate to:

1. Operational workflow improvement
2. Sales-flow improvement

Changes should reduce friction without unnecessarily expanding scope.

## Internal Experience

Dashboard design should favor:

* Broad visibility
* Clear trend recognition
* Passive discrepancy indicators
* Traceable system activity
* Role-based action controls

## Consistency

Common components should remain consistent across:

* Homepage
* Category pages
* Product listings
* Product-detail pages
* Cart
* Account
* Reorder
* Internal dashboard
